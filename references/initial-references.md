Excellent haul. Here's a clean map of what's citable and what's useful:

**Core Mathematical References — definitely cite these:**

- **Cooper & Kennedy (1990)** — "A Generating Function for the Distribution of the Scores of all Possible Bowling Games," *Mathematics Magazine*, 63, 239-243. This is THE foundational paper — Wolfram MathWorld cites it, the Balmoral site references it. Your work extends it to World Bowling. Essential cite.

- **Hohn, J.L. (2009)** — "Generalized Probabilistic Bowling Distributions," Masters Thesis, Western Kentucky University. Extends Cooper & Kennedy to N-frame, M-pin games. Good prior work to cite and position against.

- **Shannon, C. (1948)** — "A Mathematical Theory of Communication," *Bell System Technical Journal*. The entropy calculation foundation. Standard cite.

**Hot Hand Literature — directly relevant to recency bias section:**

- **Gilovich, Vallone & Tversky (1985)** — "The Hot Hand in Basketball," *Cognitive Psychology*, 17, 295-314. The foundational paper on misperception of streaks. Classic cite for the recency bias argument.

- **Yaari & Eisenmann (2012)** — "Hot Hand on Strike: Bowling Data Indicates Correlation to Recent Past Results, Not Causality," *PLOS ONE*. This is bowling-specific and peer-reviewed — perfect fit. Full text available free on PMC.

- **Dorsey-Palmateer & Smith (2004)** — "Bowlers' Hot Hands," *The American Statistician*, 58, 38-45. Another bowling-specific hot hand paper, cited in the statistical analysis paper.

**Scoring System Design — good context:**

- **Point Score Systems and Competitive Imbalance in Professional Soccer** (ResearchGate) — directly addresses scoring rule design trade-offs. The three-point rule literature in soccer is a close analogue to your argument.

- **Shannon Entropy and Herfindahl-Hirschman Index as Team Performance Indicators** (Ausloos, 2023), *Entropy* MDPI journal, PMC full text available. Directly uses Shannon entropy in a sports competition context — validates your methodological approach and is very recent.

- **Application of entropy measures to analysis of performance in team sports**, *International Journal of Performance Analysis in Sport*, 16(2). Broader entropy-in-sports literature.

**The Statistical Bowling Paper — worth reading carefully:**

The Maynooth University paper (mural.maynoothuniversity.ie) on statistical analysis of 1240 bowling games is interesting — it studies real player score distributions and handicapping fairness. Its reference list alone is gold — it cites Cooper & Kennedy, Hohn, and Dorsey-Palmateer all in one place. Fetch that paper's full reference list in Claude Code.

**What's missing — worth one more search:**

- Proper scoring rules literature (Brier, Savage) — formalises exactly your argument about what scoring systems measure
- Markov model bowling paper (VanDerwerken mentioned in Semantic Scholar) — may be citable

**The gap your paper fills:**
None of these papers compare traditional vs World Bowling scoring mathematically. Cooper & Kennedy only do traditional. The hot hand papers study whether streaks exist, not whether scoring systems reward them. Your paper is genuinely new in combining: exact enumeration of both systems + sequence sensitivity as a formal property + scoring system design argument. That's the contribution statement right there.
